Strategic Reality Check: Is International Business Events Worth It?

PCMA Customer Advisory Summit, 17–20 Nov 202629 Oct 2026B Mac, PCMA advisor

The program asks which events Ixtapa-Zihuatanejo can win, not whether winning them pays. At realistic scale, business events are a low-single-digit share of room nights, so the case rests on timing and rate.

Evidence and limits

The economics are illustrative and gross. State figures are press releases, not audited statistics, and the destination's own baseline (weekday occupancy, group room nights, lead and lost-business data) is still missing.

Bottom line

Five concerns are the right questions, and the summit's design does not ask them. The program asks which events Ixtapa-Zihuatanejo can win, not whether winning them pays. On the evidence so far, the concerns hold.

  • No baseline: confirmed. The hard recent series is airport traffic: international fell 8.8% in 2025 and 8.9% more in Jan–Sep 2026. A state-reported 2025 occupancy of 65.5% exists but is unreconciled. Seasonal figures are press releases, and the statistics offered on the call are 10–15 years old.
  • The "why": not articulated on the call. The hosts voiced belief and enthusiasm, not a goal or a metric.
  • Implications: heavy and largely unexamined. International events need lift, incentive-grade rooms, working space, a local host base and years of sales effort. All of Mexico hosts about 191 ICCA congresses a year across 36 cities.
  • The prize: modest. An ambitious domestic-led program of 30 events a year is about 1–2% of sold room nights. An international-led version is smaller still. (Illustrative; assumptions in the economics section.)
  • Leisure alternative: unresolved for lack of data. But air, a safety proof, data and product quality serve both segments, so meetings' incremental cost may be mostly the sales layer.

Recommended approach: support the goal, ask the hosts to state the "why" and the bar, then stage-gate: a 60-day baseline, then a domestic and incentive pilot, then international only if the pilot clears the bar.

Do they know why?

Not on the evidence of the call. The hosts stated belief and enthusiasm; nobody stated a goal, a number or a trade-off.

What the call showed:

  • The framing: Eduardo described the aim as converting a successful leisure destination into a meetings, incentive and Latin American association destination, and helping it "internationalize" what it already does in the Mexican market.
  • The hosts: the municipal tourism director said he believes in the segment. The bureau director said the industry and government work together. The international promotion manager hedged on who started the initiative.
  • What was missing: a target in room nights, months or revenue, and any mention of what would be given up to fund it.

Candidate whys, each with the metric that would prove it:

Candidate "why" Metric that would show success What the evidence says now
Fill the midweek and shoulder trough Net incremental Sunday–Thursday room nights in November and May–October Weekend peak readings are 81–95%; weekday and shoulder data are unpublished. Annual occupancy is state-reported at 65.5%, and one report puts summer 2025 at 74%

A good answer sounds like this: "By 2029, X net incremental midweek room nights in named months, at an ADR of at least Y, from Z source markets, at a cost under W per room night." A weak answer is "to become a meetings destination."

Competitive reality:

  • The national field is thin and concentrated. All of Mexico hosted about 191 ICCA congresses in 36 cities (Publimetro, Aug 2026), about five a city on average. Mexico City, Guadalajara, Monterrey and Cancún lead (Forbes México, older).
  • Cancún's center alone hosted 19 congresses in 2025, seven of them world-level (News in America).
  • The hosts benchmark against Puerto Vallarta and said it has better connectivity (call).
  • Time horizons differ: association bids are typically won two to five years out (general knowledge), against a promotion horizon the hosts have not stated.
  • One possible channel: COMIR and Sectur are forming a national convention bureau (Revista Equipar). Ask whether Ixtapa is engaged.

Is the incremental contribution worth it?

At realistic scale, business events are a low-single-digit slice of the destination's room nights. Their value is in timing and rate, not volume. These scenarios are illustrative and gross.

Scenario Events a year × delegates Room nights Share of sold room nights Gross delegate spend Share of one season's state-reported spillover
Modest, domestic 15 × 150, 2.5 nights 5,063 0.3–0.6% US$1.9M (about MXN 37M) 3%
Ambitious, domestic 30 × 200, 3 nights 16,200 1.0–2.1% US$5.1M (about MXN 99M) 8%
Conditional band, domestic 3 × 600, 3 nights 4,860 0.3–0.6% US$1.5M (about MXN 30M) 3%
International-led 5 × 200, 3.5 nights 3,150 0.2–0.4% US$2.2M (about MXN 43M) 4%

Assumptions:

  • Rooms: 0.9 rooms per delegate (a general planning norm).
  • Base: 0.8–1.6 million sold room nights a year, from 3,300–6,500 rooms at the state-reported 65.5% annual occupancy (geography unverified).
  • Spend: US$850 per domestic participant and US$2,219 per international congress participant (Sectur, 2022, via Vocero); about MXN 19.5 per US$.
  • Comparison: one season's spillover of MXN 1,177 million (year-end 2024–25, state-reported), not the whole year.

How to read it:

  • Domestic beats international on return. The international-led case yields less because event counts are low and bids are slow.
  • These figures are gross. Only room nights that would otherwise sit empty are incremental. Weekday and shoulder data decide that, and they are missing.
  • The spend premium may be smaller here. Sectur's US$850 is about 1.7–1.9 times the state's leisure spend per tourist, but the bases differ and all-inclusive guests already pay for a full stay. Claims of "six times" are promotional.
  • The hotel captures most delegate spend. The call described a modest local economy, so leakage to local businesses is uncertain.
  • Stays are short. An older Sectur study (2010 data) put corporate meetings at about 2.3 nights, so room nights run lower than leisure.
  • Against annual activity the prize is smaller still. A reported MXN 21.6 billion of 2025 tourism economic activity (RTG, via the companion briefing; unreconciled) would put these scenarios at about 0.2–0.5% of it.
  • The cost side is unknown. Ask for the bureau's budget, headcount and leisure-versus-meetings split, then compute cost per net incremental room night.

Could the money be better spent on leisure?

It depends on marginal return per peso, and nobody can compute that today. The evidence suggests the two compete less than they appear to on shared investments, and more on sales effort.

  • Shared investments: air route support, a planner-grade safety proof, a data system, and product quality serve leisure and meetings alike. The U.S. advisory text hits leisure too, and Canada and the U.S. are about 40% of visitors.
  • Meetings-only costs: a bid desk, trade shows such as IMEX America and IBTM, familiarization trips, bid funds, English-language materials, working-capacity upgrades.
  • Leisure's weak spots: international traffic fell 8.8% in 2025 and another 8.9% in Jan–Sep 2026, peak winter was flat, and every published seasonal occupancy reading is a weekend or holiday snapshot. Those are leisure problems that leisure money could address.
  • The counter-case for meetings: Sectur argues meetings steady low-season occupancy (Vocero). That is true only if a weekday and shoulder trough exists, and the hosts have not shown it.

A decision test the hosts could run on three options:

Option Metric Data needed
Meetings sales program Cost per net incremental room night; payback time Bureau budget; group and weekday occupancy data
Leisure shoulder and midweek promotion Cost per net incremental room night Campaign spend; monthly occupancy
Airline route support Cost per added seat and per room night Seat schedules; route load factors

The third way is a domestic corporate and incentive pilot of 100–300 delegates, using existing inventory with no new facilities and a small sales layer. If the pilot clears the bar, scale it. If not, the money goes back to leisure, and the baseline work still pays off.

A staged path

The aim is to turn "we believe in this" into a bar the hosts set themselves.

Gate What happens Proposed decision rule (the hosts set the numbers)
0. Baseline, within 60 days A one-page view: monthly and weekday occupancy, group room nights by source market, the RFP and lead log, the bureau's budget split, and a written "why" Proceed only if a weekday or shoulder trough is shown
1. Domestic and incentive pilot, 12 months Three to five named accounts, 100–300 delegates, midweek in shoulder months Net incremental room nights, ADR and cost per room night against the leisure benchmark
2. International, only if Gate 1 clears One or two targeted corporate or incentive pursuits; association bids only with a local host-base partner Air, safety evidence and working-space checks in place first

The small yes: a one-page baseline plus a written "why" and bar, within 60 days, before real budget is committed.
